JPN
A photographic project by Alessandro Lana

The project

“During my first journey to Japan, as I contemplated the continually changing shadow of a tree cast on the white surface of a temple, a question arose naturally:

How can a photograph preserve the sense of light’s transience—the way it dances in the wind, continually changing in form and intensity as a cloud passes or the branches sway, drawing me into a certain state of mind—without destroying its very nature by fixing it in a static and therefore entirely ineffective representation?

From this came the idea of an apparatus rather than a representation: one capable of generating a new system of images each time, always different, always equally elusive.”

JPN is a black-and-white photographic work by Alessandro Lana, shot on film and printed by hand in the darkroom. Built on superimposition and perception, the images overlap and dissolve into one another, shifting between presence and absence, clarity and disappearance.

The space in between

JPN through the eyes of acclaimed photographer Renato D’Agostin.

“Japan has long inspired countless attempts to define its essence through symbols, traditions and rituals. Yet what continues to distinguish the experience of the country is something far less tangible: the space that exists between things.

It is found in the silence that seems to inhabit certain spaces, in the brief hesitation of a passerby, in the reflection of light across a polished surface, or in the way one shadow quietly dissolves into another. These are moments that resist certainty. They cannot be possessed, only encountered.

Photography is often understood as a means of preserving what is visible. Yet its deeper potential may lie elsewhere: in making perceptible what is fleeting, ambiguous and easily overlooked.
Rather than explaining a place, an image can invite a different way of seeing—one that embraces incompleteness and allows mystery to remain intact.

JPN offers this rare condition. It encourages a slower gaze, where absence carries as much presence as form, and where every detail seems suspended between permanence and disappearance. The ordinary becomes quietly extraordinary, not through spectacle, but through attention.

Fragments of encounters, traces of passing moments, and reflections of a landscape that continuously transforms itself through light, time and perception. An invitation to look with greater patience—to discover the quiet poetry that so often resides within the unnoticed.”

— Renato D’Agostin

The process
[Video]

JPN was shot entirely on film.

From the negatives, silver gelatin prints were then produced by hand in the darkroom. Each frame is approached individually, in an organic and intuitive process that draws out contrast, detail and chiaroscuro through the interplay of light.

The grain that marks the images is an intrinsic part of this artisanal practice, lending each print a singular character.

Bio

Alessandro Lana (b. 1994) is a photographer and videographer based near Venice.

He collaborates with The Venice Venice Hotel, The Erose, Venice M’Art, G124 Renzo Piano, Fondazione Renzo Piano and the University of Padua, among others.

JPN is his first authorial project.
